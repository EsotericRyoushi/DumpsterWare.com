14 November 2026 Site

Three divisions, one workshop

The site used to be one long page with everything stacked on it: games, an app, a conduit calculator. It worked, but it made DumpsterWare look like a pile of unrelated things instead of a studio with a few interests.

So the site is now split into four places that share one header: Company for the studio, Games for the sandbox work, Apps for family software, and Tools for the trade tools. Same voice, clearer rooms.

This round also added a proper page for Project: Sandbox — the life-sim sandbox that has been a single card for too long — plus this devlog, a sitemap, and a share card so links don't look bare when someone posts one.

None of that is a launch. It's just the workshop getting tidier before the next thing gets built.

2 November 2026 Games

What "no finish line" actually means

Every game has an ending, even the ones that pretend not to. A credits roll, a final boss, a number that stops going up. When we say Project: Sandbox has no finish line, we mean something narrower and harder: there is no state the game is trying to push you toward.

That changes the design. A quest that exists only to unlock the next quest starts to feel like a hallway. A job that's fun on day one but pointless on day thirty is a treadmill with better art. So the question we keep asking is: would this still be interesting if it never led anywhere?

It's why the trades matter more than the plot, why the town keeps its own hours, and why "pick a different job on Tuesday" is a feature rather than a failure to commit. The game should be a place, not a funnel.

We're still early. But that's the standard everything gets held to.
