The idea

A place to live, not a level to beat.

Most games hand you a goal and then a wall to hit when you reach it. Red Sands starts somewhere else: a world that doesn't need you, and a job you can be good at.

You drop in, pick up a trade, and make a living. Chop wood, haul a load, build something that lasts. The point isn't to finish — it's to have another day worth showing up for.

It's still early. The world, the trades, and the pacing are all in motion, and we're in no hurry to lock them down before they're ready.

The loop

Pick a trade. Build a routine. Keep going.

Three ideas we keep coming back to while the systems take shape.

01

Pick up a trade

A good job should feel like a skill, not a button. The work is the game — learning it, getting faster at it, and knowing when to take a different one.

02

Make a living

Money matters, but it isn't the score. A living is a roof, a routine, and enough slack to say yes to something else on a Tuesday.

03

Stay a while

No credits, no final boss, no correct way to play. Five minutes or five hours — the world should be happy to see you either way.

What we're building toward

Systems that get out of the way.

A working list, not a promise list. It will change as the game teaches us what it wants to be.

Design goals

A world with its own hours

Days should pass whether or not you're watching. That means routines, weather, and a town that keeps its own schedule — so coming back after a week feels like coming back, not reloading a level.

  • Trades with a learning curve, not a grind wall
  • A home worth shaping, one small decision at a time
  • Saves that respect the time you put in
  • No daily chores disguised as content
  • Life sim
  • Sandbox
  • Endless

On purpose

What's not in it.

Just as important as the feature list.

01

No finish line

No credits, no end-game screen, no "you beat the game" moment. The world is the point, not the exit.

02

No correct way to play

You can be the neighbour who fixes fences or the one who never leaves the workshop. Both are valid lives.

03

No rush to ship

We'd rather get the systems right than hit a date we invented for ourselves. It ships when it's worth your evening.
